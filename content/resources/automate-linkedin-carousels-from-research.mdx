# How to create LinkedIn carousels from current research

LinkedIn carousels are one of the highest-performing formats for personal-brand content. The problem is that each carousel demands three separate skills: research, copywriting, and design. Most solo operators burn an entire evening on a single post.

This guide shows you how to compress that into a repeatable workflow.

## Start with a focused topic

Choose one timely topic. The tighter the focus, the better the result. Good examples:

- A new AI model release and what it means for automation
- A cybersecurity breach and what operators should learn from it
- A cloud pricing change and how small teams should respond

Avoid broad prompts like "write about AI." Broad inputs produce generic outputs.

## Collect current sources

Use a research tool to find recent reporting. Firecrawl is a strong fit because it returns clean, structured source data from current pages.

Limit yourself to five to seven sources. More sources create noise. Fewer sources create thin output.

## Pick one story

Read through the sources and choose the single most useful story for your audience. Ask:

- Is this relevant to the people who follow me?
- Can I add a practical takeaway?
- Is it timely enough to post today or this week?

If the answer to any question is no, pick a different story.

## Write four content slides

A five-slide carousel works best when the first four slides deliver value and the fifth slide asks for a follow.

Use this structure:

1. **Hook:** what changed and why it matters
2. **Context:** the signal behind the headline
3. **Takeaway:** what the reader should understand
4. **Action:** what the reader should do next

Keep each slide under thirty words. Carousel text should be scannable.

## Design the slides

Render each slide as a complete PNG image. Use a consistent visual system: dark background, accent color, readable font, small logo or handle.

The fifth slide is your subscription slide. It should be deterministic, not generated, so your brand identity stays intact every time.

## Review before publishing

This is the most important step. AI drafts fast, but you are the final editor. Read every slide, check every source, edit the commentary, and only then confirm the publish action.

## What this workflow looks like in BrandOps

BrandOps automates the research, writing, and design steps and presents the finished carousel for your review. You keep the publish button.
