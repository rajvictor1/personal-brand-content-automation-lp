# How to create LinkedIn carousels from current research

LinkedIn carousels are one of the highest-performing content formats for personal brands. The challenge is that each carousel demands three separate skills: research, copywriting, and visual design. Most solo operators spend an entire evening creating one post.

This guide shows you a faster, repeatable workflow.

## What you will learn

- How to pick a timely, focused topic
- How to collect recent sources in minutes
- How to write four value slides and one subscription slide
- How to design the carousel as image assets
- Why review must happen before publishing

## Step 1: Choose one focused topic

A focused topic produces a better carousel than a broad one.

Good topics:

- A new AI model release and what it means for operators
- A cybersecurity breach and the practical lesson
- A cloud pricing change and how small teams should respond

Avoid broad prompts like "write about AI." Broad inputs create generic output.

## Step 2: Collect current sources

Use a research tool that returns clean, recent web data. The goal is five to seven trusted sources.

Why current sources matter:

- LinkedIn rewards timely content
- Your audience trusts cited information
- You reduce the risk of outdated advice

Limit your sources. More sources create noise. Fewer sources create thin output.

## Step 3: Pick the single best story

Read through your sources and choose one story your audience actually cares about. Ask three questions:

1. Is this relevant to the people who follow me?
2. Can I add a practical takeaway?
3. Is it timely enough to post this week?

If the answer to any question is no, pick a different story.

## Step 4: Write four content slides

A five-slide carousel works best when the first four slides deliver value and the fifth slide asks for a follow.

Use this slide structure:

| Slide | Purpose | Length |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Slide 1 | Hook: what changed and why it matters | Under 15 words |
| Slide 2 | Context: the signal behind the headline | Under 25 words |
| Slide 3 | Takeaway: what the reader should understand | Under 25 words |
| Slide 4 | Action: what the reader should do next | Under 25 words |
| Slide 5 | Follow: subscription or CTA | Under 10 words |

Keep text scannable. Use short sentences, line breaks, and bold phrases.

## Step 5: Design the slides

Render each slide as a complete PNG image. Use a consistent visual system:

- Dark or branded background
- One accent color
- Large, readable font
- Small logo or handle

The fifth slide should be a deterministic subscription slide. Do not generate it from scratch each time. Use a fixed template so your brand identity stays consistent.

## Step 6: Review before publishing

This is the most important step. AI drafts fast, but you own the final output.

Check every slide for:

- Accuracy against your sources
- Tone that matches your brand
- No em dashes or awkward phrasing if you follow a strict style guide
- Correct spelling, names, and numbers

Only confirm publish after review.

## FAQ

### What makes a LinkedIn carousel perform well?

A strong hook in the first slide, scannable text, a clear takeaway, and a subscription slide at the end.

### How many slides should a LinkedIn carousel have?

Five slides is the sweet spot: four value slides and one follow slide.

### Can I publish a carousel without reviewing it?

You can, but you should not. Review-first publishing protects your brand from mistakes, misinformation, and off-brand tone.

## Key takeaway

AI can research, write, and design the carousel. You still make the editorial decision to publish.
