You do not need to be a designer to create LinkedIn carousels that look professional and get read. You need a repeatable structure and a few simple rules.

This guide gives you the 5-slide LinkedIn carousel formula, word counts for each slide, and a pre-publish checklist. It is designed for founders, trainers, consultants, and solo operators who want consistent carousel quality without hiring a designer.

## Key Takeaways

- The best carousels follow a clear five-slide structure: hook, three proof points, follow.
- Each slide should contain one idea and no more than 35 words.
- Visual consistency matters more than visual complexity.
- White space, readable fonts, and a single accent color beat cluttered design.
- BrandOps generates carousels using this exact 5-slide formula.

## The 5-Slide LinkedIn Carousel Formula

### Slide 1: The Hook

The first slide decides whether the reader swipes. It must answer one question: why should I care?

**Purpose:** stop the scroll and promise value.

**Structure:**

- One bold statement or question
- Optional sub-line that adds specificity

**Word count:** 8 to 20 words.

**Examples:**

- "Most AI content sounds the same. Here is how to fix it."
- "3 mistakes that kill LinkedIn carousel engagement."
- "You don't need more tools. You need clearer workflows."

### Slides 2-4: The Proof

These three slides deliver on the hook. Each slide should make one point, support it briefly, and lead naturally to the next.

**Purpose:** educate, persuade, or prove the hook.

**Structure per slide:**

- One short heading
- One or two supporting sentences
- Optional stat or example

**Word count per slide:** 20 to 35 words.

**Examples:**

- Slide 2: "Start with a current source. Outdated examples make your opinion feel generic."
- Slide 3: "Add your angle. The same data becomes valuable when you interpret it."
- Slide 4: "End the slide with a takeaway the reader can use immediately."

### Slide 5: The Follow

The final slide tells the reader what to do next. It is also where you build your email list, prompt a comment, or invite a follow.

**Purpose:** convert attention into action.

**Structure:**

- One-line summary
- Clear call to action
- Optional resource link or newsletter sign-up prompt

**Word count:** 15 to 30 words.

**Examples:**

- "Save this for your next carousel. Follow for more frameworks like this."
- "Want the full workflow? Join the newsletter linked in my profile."
- "Which slide is hardest for you? Comment below and I will reply."

## LinkedIn Carousel Dimensions and Format

- **Recommended size:** 1080 x 1350 pixels per slide.
- **Aspect ratio:** 4:5 for maximum mobile real estate.
- **File format:** PNG for text-heavy slides, PDF if LinkedIn allows PDF carousels in your region.
- **Slide count:** 5 to 10 slides. Five is the sweet spot for completion rate.

## Design Rules for Non-Designers

### Use one font family

Pick one clean sans-serif font and use it for everything. Limit yourself to two weights: bold for headings, regular for body text.

### Use one accent color

Choose one brand color for headings, icons, and highlights. Use black, white, and gray for everything else.

### Leave white space

Never fill a slide edge to edge. White space makes text readable and the slide feel premium.

### Keep text large

Body text should be at least 32 pixels on a 1080-wide canvas. Headings should be 48 pixels or larger.

### Use contrast

Light text on dark background, or dark text on light background. Avoid mid-tone backgrounds behind text.

### Repeat visual elements

Use the same icon style, shape, and placement on every slide. Repetition creates visual rhythm.

## Pre-Publish Checklist

Before uploading a carousel, check these items:

- [ ] Slide 1 clearly promises value
- [ ] Slides 2-4 each make one point
- [ ] Slide 5 has one clear call to action
- [ ] No slide exceeds 35 words
- [ ] Text is large enough to read on mobile
- [ ] Color contrast is strong
- [ ] Visual style is consistent across all slides
- [ ] File exports at 1080 x 1350 pixels
- [ ] Spelling and grammar checked
- [ ] The CTA matches your current goal

## Common Mistakes

### Putting too much text on one slide

If a slide needs more than 35 words, split it into two slides or simplify the idea.

### Forgetting the follow slide

A carousel without a CTA wastes attention. Always end with a clear next step.

### Using inconsistent design

Every slide should look like it belongs to the same carousel. Same fonts, same colors, same shapes.

### Hiding the hook

The hook belongs on slide 1, not buried in slide 3. Lead with the strongest line.

## FAQ

### Do I need design skills to make LinkedIn carousels?

No. A simple structure, clean fonts, and consistent colors are enough to look professional.

### What is the best number of slides for a LinkedIn carousel?

Five slides is ideal for most topics. It is short enough to finish and long enough to deliver value.

### How do I choose colors if I don't have a brand palette?

Pick one color you like, one dark neutral, and one light neutral. Use the color for headings and accents only.

### Should I use images or just text on slides?

Text-first carousels perform well on LinkedIn because they are easy to read quickly. Use icons or simple shapes rather than stock photos.

A strong LinkedIn carousel is more about structure than design talent. Use the 5-slide formula, keep text short, and stay visually consistent. If you follow the framework, your carousels will look professional and perform reliably.
