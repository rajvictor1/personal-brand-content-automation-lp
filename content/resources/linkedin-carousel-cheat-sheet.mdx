# LinkedIn Carousel Cheat Sheet

Use this cheat sheet to build a five-slide LinkedIn carousel from any single research topic.

## The five-slide structure

| Slide | Purpose | Max words | What to include |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Hook | 12 | The change and why it matters right now |
| 2 | Cause | 20 | What drove the change |
| 3 | Implication | 25 | What it means for your specific audience |
| 4 | Action | 20 | One concrete step the reader can take |
| 5 | Follow | 15 | One line + a connection or comment prompt |

## Slide-level rules

- Total carousel copy should be under 100 words.
- One idea per slide.
- Use short sentences. No paragraphs.
- Avoid jargon unless your audience expects it.
- Every claim must tie back to a source from your research step.

## Design rules

- Use one consistent template.
- Keep background simple so text stays readable on mobile.
- Limit each slide to one accent color.
- Use the same font pairing across all five slides.

## Pre-publish checklist

- [ ] The hook is clear in the first 3 seconds.
- [ ] Every claim has a source.
- [ ] The action step is specific, not generic.
- [ ] The follow slide asks for a low-friction response.
- [ ] Images are high-resolution PNGs.
- [ ] The tone matches your personal brand.

## Quick formula

**Slide 1:** [Trend or change] is happening.

**Slide 2:** It was caused by [driver].

**Slide 3:** This means [audience] will face [outcome].

**Slide 4:** Do this: [specific action].

**Slide 5:** Want more? Follow or comment below.
